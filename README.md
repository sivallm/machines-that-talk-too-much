# Machines That Talk Too Much  
## How Language Models Learned to Predict the Next Word

This repository contains the source for an open book that explains how
language models evolved over time — from early statistical methods to
modern large language models.

The focus is not hype or speculation, but **how things actually work**:
how data, training methods, evaluation, and inference systems changed
over decades, and why predicting the next word turned out to be enough
to make machines appear intelligent.

The book is written for a **general audience**.
No prior AI or ML background is required.

---

## What This Book Covers

- Early language models and rule-based systems  
- The rise of statistical NLP and n-grams  
- Neural networks, RNNs, and sequence models  
- Transformers and scaling laws  
- Data evolution: size, quality, and filtering  
- Training evolution: objectives, alignment, fine-tuning  
- Testing and evaluation: benchmarks vs reality  
- Inference evolution: latency, cost, and deployment constraints  
- Why “next-word prediction” works — and where it fails  

---

## What This Book Is Not

- Not a tutorial on using ChatGPT or APIs  
- Not vendor-specific  
- Not a promise of artificial general intelligence  
- Not a math-heavy ML textbook  

---

## Structure

The book is organized chronologically.
Each chapter represents a shift in:
- data
- training strategy
- evaluation method
- inference constraint

Humor is intentional. Anthropomorphism is not.
