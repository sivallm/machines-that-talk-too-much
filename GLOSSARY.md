# Glossary
Machines That Talk Too Much

This glossary defines terms as they are used in this book.
Definitions are intentionally plain and non-technical.

If a term appears in the text, it MUST match these meanings.
No alternative definitions are allowed.

---

## language model
A system that produces text by estimating which word is likely to come next,
based on patterns learned from large amounts of text.

---

## large language model
A language model trained on very large datasets using neural networks,
typically with many parameters and high computational cost.

---

## token
A unit of text used internally by a language model.
A token may be a whole word, part of a word, or punctuation.

---

## predicting the next word
The core operation of a language model:
selecting the most likely next token given the text so far.

This phrase describes behavior, not intent or understanding.

---

## training
The process of adjusting a model’s parameters so that its predictions
better match examples in the training data.

---

## training data
The collection of text used during training.
Training data does not equal knowledge or truth.

---

## data scale
A description of how much training data is used,
often measured in tokens rather than documents.

---

## fine-tuning
Additional training performed after the initial training phase,
usually to adjust behavior or performance on specific tasks.

---

## behavior shaping
Techniques used to influence how a trained model responds,
without changing the underlying objective.

---

## evaluation
The process of measuring how a model performs on defined tasks or datasets.

---

## benchmark
A standardized dataset or task used during evaluation.
Benchmarks approximate behavior; they do not define capability.

---

## inference
The phase where a trained model is used to produce text.
Inference involves trade-offs between speed, cost, and output quality.

---

## deployment
Making a model available for real-world use,
including infrastructure, cost, and operational constraints.

---

## latency
The time it takes for a model to produce an output after receiving input.

---

## scope
The boundary of topics the book intentionally covers.
Topics outside scope may be mentioned briefly but are not explored.

---

## anthropomorphism
Describing machines using human mental states or intentions.
This book avoids anthropomorphism by design.
